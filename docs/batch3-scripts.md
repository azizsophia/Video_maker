### 1. The Night the Moon Split (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 54:1]
"Of all the miracles Muslims believe in... this is the one people find hardest
to say out loud. The moon... the actual moon... split in two. And the men who
saw it never stopped telling it."
Visual: full moon huge over a dark desert horizon.

2. TITLE CARD - THE MOON / the night it split (deep read)
"This is the night... the moon was split."
Visual: slow drifting clouds crossing a bright moon.

3. (context)
"Makkah, before the migration. The Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, had
been preaching for years, and the leaders of his city wanted proof. So they
demanded a sign... something no man could fake. Not words. The sky."
Visual: rugged dark mountains under deep night.

4. [Sahih al-Bukhari 4867]
"The people of Makkah asked him to show them a sign... and he showed them the
splitting of the moon. That demand, and what followed it, sits in Sahih
al-Bukhari... the most rigorously authenticated book in Islam after the
Qur'an."
Visual: star-filled sky, one bright object rising.

5. [Sahih al-Bukhari 3869]
"Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, was standing there. We
were with the Prophet at Mina, he said... a valley just outside Makkah. And
the moon was split. One piece went toward the mountain. And the
Prophet said... bear witness. Be witnesses to this."
Visual: moonlight over a jagged mountain ridge.

6. QUOTE - Qur'an 54:1 (shown)
"And the Qur'an itself carries the event, in a chapter named The Moon. The
Hour has come near... and the moon has split."
Visual: the moon reflected, trembling, on dark water.

7. (reflection)
"Notice the order of that verse. Not... look what happened. But... the Hour
has come near. The split moon was never just a spectacle. It was a clock,
striking once... reminding every eye that looked up that this world has an
end."
Visual: slow clouds swallowing the moon into darkness.

8. CLOSE (reflection)
"The same moon hangs over you tonight... whole, quiet, patient. The men who
saw it torn in two changed their lives. It has never needed to happen twice."
Visual: calm night landscape, moonlit, vast and still.

### 2. The Test of Jerusalem (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sahih al-Bukhari 3886]
"They thought they finally had him. He claimed he had traveled, in one night,
to a city a month's ride away... a city they knew, and he had never lived in.
So they demanded he describe it. And he described it... looking at something
they could not see."
Visual: ancient stone city walls at night, lamplight.

2. TITLE CARD - THE TEST / the night journey, examined (deep read)
"This is the test... of Jerusalem."
Visual: slow mist moving over dark ancient stone.

3. (context)
"Muslims believe that in one night, the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him,
was taken by Allah from Makkah to Bayt al-Maqdis... the holy sanctuary in
Jerusalem... and then raised through the heavens. It is called the Night
Journey. And when he told his city the next morning... they laughed. Two
months of caravan riding, there and back... in a night?"
Visual: long empty desert road under stars.

4. (context)
"But here was their opening. Makkah's caravans had ridden north into Sham...
greater Syria... for generations, and some of those merchants had seen
Jerusalem with their own eyes. They knew its shape. If this man had never
been there... the details would break him."
Visual: aerial of old stone city, golden and worn.

5. [Sahih al-Bukhari 3886]
"He described it himself. Standing in al-Hijr... the open enclosure beside
the Ka'bah, the ancient sanctuary at the heart of Makkah... he said... when
Quraysh, his own tribe, did not believe me... Allah displayed Jerusalem in
front of me. And I began describing it to them... while I was looking at
it."
Visual: shaft of light falling into an ancient courtyard.

6. [Sahih Muslim 170]
"The same account stands in Sahih Muslim. Allah lifted before me Bayt
al-Maqdis... and I narrated its signs... while I was looking at it. Sign after
sign... detail after detail... from a man staring past them at something
only he could see."
Visual: dark room, one window of brilliant light.

7. (reflection)
"The examiners chose the test. The examiners knew the answers. And the record
of what happened next is not that the details failed... it is that the
description held."
Visual: empty ancient stone street at dawn, silent.

8. CLOSE (reflection)
"Fourteen centuries later, the two cities are still standing... still joined
by that one night. He passed their test. The question the story leaves
behind... is yours."
Visual: sunrise breaking over old city rooftops.

### 3. Barefoot Shepherds, Tall Towers (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sahih Muslim 8]
"Fourteen hundred years ago, a question was asked in a small mud-brick city...
when is the end of the world? The answer pointed at shepherds... and said,
watch what they build."
Visual: vast desert dunes, a distant herd, no faces.

2. TITLE CARD - THE TOWERS / a sign given to Umar (deep read)
"This is the sign... of the towers."
Visual: slow low clouds drifting across black sky.

3. (context)
"The scene is one of the most famous in Islam. A stranger in pure white walks
into the gathering of the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him... no dust of
travel on him, though no one knows his face. He sits knee to knee with the
Prophet and begins to examine him. The companion Umar ibn al-Khattab, may
Allah be pleased with him, was watching... and narrated every word."
Visual: wind moving over empty desert at dusk.

4. [Sahih Muslim 8]
"The stranger asks... tell me about the Hour, the end of everything. The
Prophet, peace be upon him, answers... the one asked knows no more than the
asker. Even he did not know the day. But signs... signs he was given."
Visual: hourglass-free image: long shadows stretching over sand.

5. [Sahih Muslim 8]
"And then, this. You will find barefooted, destitute goat herders... vying
with one another... in the construction of magnificent buildings. The
stranger, the Prophet then revealed, was the angel Jibril... Gabriel...
who had come to teach them their religion."
Visual: gleaming skyscrapers rising, low angle, night.

6. (context)
"Sit with the odds of that sentence. It names the poorest people of the
poorest trade in a mud-brick land... and stakes the claim that THEY, not
Rome, not Persia... would one day vie with one another over magnificent
towers."
Visual: cranes over a glittering skyline at dusk.

7. (reflection)
"Today the deserts of Arabia hold the tallest tower ever built... raised
within living memory of grandfathers who herded goats barefoot. The sign was
not given to frighten. It was given so that, when you saw it... you would
remember who said it."
Visual: a needle-like supertall tower piercing clouds.

8. CLOSE (reflection)
"A prophecy is a message addressed to the generation that watches it come
true. This one... was addressed to yours."
Visual: city lights spreading to the horizon at night.

### 4. The Lizard's Hole (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sahih al-Bukhari 7320]
"The Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, once described his own followers'
future... and it was not flattering. He said they would copy the nations
before them... so exactly... that no hole would be too small to crawl into."
Visual: cracked desert earth, a dark burrow entrance.

2. TITLE CARD - SPAN BY SPAN / a warning to his own people (deep read)
"This is the warning... of the lizard's hole."
Visual: slow sand drifting across dark stone.

3. (context)
"First, the setting. The Qur'an and the Prophet, peace be upon him, speak
often of the nations before Islam... communities given guidance, who then
drifted, divided, and traded their religion piece by piece for fashion and
power. The warning was that his own people were not immune."
Visual: ruins of an ancient city, wind-blown.

4. [Sahih al-Bukhari 7320]
"You will follow the ways of those nations who were before you... he said...
span by span, and cubit by cubit... inch by inch... so much so that even if
they entered a hole of a lizard, you would follow them."
Visual: single-file footprints crossing endless sand.

5. [Sahih al-Bukhari 7320]
"The companions asked... do you mean the Jews and the Christians? He said...
whom else? Not spoken in contempt... the earlier nations are the only map of
how a believing people slowly becomes something else."
Visual: a worn path splitting off a straight road.

6. (reflection)
"Notice the unit of measure. Not leaps. Spans. A hand-width at a time. No
one abandons what they believe in a day... they inch away from it, each step
too small to feel like a decision."
Visual: macro of sand grains slipping over an edge.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"Fourteen centuries later, the prophecy reads like a mirror... imitation,
inch by inch, all the way down into the smallest holes of culture. The
warning was recorded so you could catch yourself mid-span. Which direction
are your inches going?"
Visual: dawn light over a long, straight desert road.

### 5. When No One Will Take Your Charity (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sahih al-Bukhari 1411]
"There is a prophecy in Sahih al-Bukhari about money. Not a crash... not
poverty. The opposite. A day when a man will carry his charity through the
streets... and come home still holding it."
Visual: coins spilling across a dark table, slow.

2. TITLE CARD - THE UNWANTED GOLD / a prophecy about wealth (deep read)
"This is the prophecy... of the unwanted gold."
Visual: slow golden dust settling on black.

3. (context)
"To feel the weight of this, remember who first heard it. Madinah was a city
of date farmers and shepherds who knew famine years by name. Charity...
zakat... is a pillar of Islam, and in that world there was never any
difficulty finding a hand open to receive it."
Visual: dry palm grove in harsh noon light.

4. [Sahih al-Bukhari 1412]
"And into that world the Prophet, peace be upon him, said... the Hour will
not be established until your wealth increases so much... that a man will be
worried... because no one will accept his zakat. The person he offers it to
will answer... I am not in need of it."
Visual: overflowing grain pouring past the edges of a bowl.

5. [Sahih al-Bukhari 1411]
"In another narration he pressed it further... give in charity now, for a
time will come when a man will wander about with his charity, and the answer
will be... if you had brought it yesterday, I would have taken it. But
today... I am not in need of it."
Visual: empty market street at golden hour.

6. (reflection)
"A prophecy about too much money, spoken to people who had almost none. No
politician promises abundance as a warning. He did... because the test of
full hands is harder than the test of empty ones."
Visual: hands-free image: light flooding an empty room.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"The instruction inside the prophecy is one word... NOW. Give now, while
someone still needs it. Generosity has an expiry date... and only Allah
knows when."
Visual: dawn light on quiet rooftops.

### 6. Wherever Night and Day Reach (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Musnad Ahmad 16957]
"When this sentence was first spoken, the entire religion fit inside one
small city. Today, no sun rises anywhere on earth without finding it. And
that... is exactly what the sentence said would happen."
Visual: timelapse of the day line moving across the earth from space-style footage.

2. TITLE CARD - NIGHT AND DAY / a prophecy the sun keeps checking (deep read)
"This is the prophecy... of night and day."
Visual: slow drift of stars fading into pre-dawn blue.

3. (context)
"Context first. Islam began with one man, the Prophet Muhammad, peace be
upon him, in Makkah... rejected by his own tribe, his followers tortured,
his message besieged. For years the religion could be erased by one bad
afternoon. That is the moment this was said."
Visual: single small campfire in an immense dark desert.

4. [Musnad Ahmad 16957]
"This matter will reach... wherever night and day reach. And Allah will not
leave a house of mud... or a house of hair... but He will cause this
religion to enter it. Houses of mud... the settled cities. Houses of hair...
the woven tents of the nomads. Meaning... everyone."
Visual: a lit village at dusk in a vast valley.

5. [Musnad Ahmad 16957]
"The narration is carried by the companion Tamim al-Dari... may Allah be
pleased with him... and recorded in the Musnad of Imam Ahmad, one of the
great early collections of the Prophet's words. The scholars whose lifework
is to test such narrations, chain by chain... judged this one authentic."
Visual: old handwritten manuscript pages turning slowly, no readable text as subject... use warm parchment texture.

6. (context)
"Now measure it. Today there are Muslims in nearly every country on
earth... roughly one in four human beings. From the tundra to the equator, the
prayer moves with the sunset, hour by hour, in an unbroken wave... night
and day, literally, cannot outrun it."
Visual: globe of city lights, slow rotation feel.

7. (reflection)
"The great empires that bet against this sentence... are gone. The sentence
is still arriving in new houses tonight."
Visual: doorway of a warm lit home under stars.

8. CLOSE (reflection)
"A man with no army, no press, and no map of the world... described the
reach of his message with the only two things that touch every home on
earth. Night... and day. Ask yourself how he knew."
Visual: sunrise flooding over the curve of a wide horizon.

### 7. The Greeting That Grew Cold (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Musnad Ahmad 3848]
"Among the great signs of the end of the world... armies, false prophets,
the sun itself... one sign is so small it hides inside an ordinary hello.
And it may already have happened to you... today."
Visual: crowded city crosswalk from above, anonymous motion.

2. TITLE CARD - THE GREETING / the smallest sign of the Hour (deep read)
"This is the sign... of the greeting."
Visual: slow bokeh of cold city lights on black.

3. [Sahih al-Bukhari 12]
"In Islam, the greeting is not small talk. As-salamu alaykum... peace be
upon you... is a prayer offered to strangers, a right owed to whoever
crosses your path. The Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, taught his
companions to spread the greeting... to those you know, and those you do
not."
Visual: warm light spilling through an old doorway.

4. [Musnad Ahmad 3848]
"And then he told them a day would come when that would quietly die. Among
the signs of the Hour... he said... is that a man will give the greeting to
another... only because he knows him."
Visual: two paths diverging in an empty park, autumn.

5. [Musnad Ahmad 3870]
"A fuller narration from the same companion... Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may
Allah be pleased with him... also judged authentic, lists what grows
alongside it... trade spreading everywhere... family ties cut...
and false testimony. A world that is more connected... and less warm...
at the same time."
Visual: endless container port or lit highways at night.

6. (reflection)
"Feel how precise that is. Not... people will become evil. Just... the
circle of your peace will shrink to the people already inside it. Warmth
rationed to acquaintances. Kindness... privatized."
Visual: one lit window in a dark apartment block.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"This is the rare prophecy you can push back on with one sentence. Peace be
upon you... said to someone who was not expecting it. The sign is that the
greeting grows cold. So warm it."
Visual: morning light warming a quiet street.

### 8. The Protected Ceiling (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 21:32]
"Right now, stones from space are hitting your roof. Tons of them, every
single day. You have never heard one land... and the reason why is written
in a fourteen hundred year old verse."
Visual: meteor streaking across a star-dense sky.

2. TITLE CARD - THE CEILING / what the Qur'an calls the sky (deep read)
"This is the verse... of the protected ceiling."
Visual: slow aurora-like glow rippling on dark sky.

3. (context)
"The Qur'an, the book Muslims believe is the speech of Allah, often points
up. To desert people sleeping under the open sky, it kept insisting that
what hung above them was not emptiness... it was architecture."
Visual: vast desert night sky over dunes, camera tilted up.

4. QUOTE - Qur'an 21:32 (shown)
"And We made the sky a protected ceiling... but they, from its signs, are
turning away."
Visual: clouds lit from above, cathedral-like light.

5. [Tafsir Ibn Kathir]
"The classical scholars read it plainly. Ibn Kathir wrote that the sky is a
roof over the earth... safe and well-guarded, raised high, protected from
anything reaching it. A guarded roof... over every head, everywhere, at
once."
Visual: sunlight bursting through towering cloudbanks.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"Today we can describe some of what that guarding looks like. A blanket of
air that burns up the daily rain of space rock into harmless streaks of
light... fields that turn away the storms of charged particles the sun throws at us.
The verse did not teach that science... it named the mercy, and left the
signs open for whoever would look."
Visual: shooting stars burning out high in the atmosphere.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"The verse ends with the ache... but they turn away from its signs. Tonight
the ceiling above you will quietly absorb a thousand small catastrophes.
Don't be the one who never once looked up and said... thank You."
Visual: still night sky over a sleeping town.

### 9. Everything Is Swimming (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 21:33]
"The Qur'an describes the sun and the moon with one strange verb. Not
shining... not rising. Swimming. And the longer you sit with that word...
the better it gets."
Visual: sun flaring low over an ocean horizon.

2. TITLE CARD - YASBAHUN / they are swimming (deep read)
"This is the verse... of the swimmers."
Visual: slow star-trail circles on deep black.

3. (context)
"Seventh century Arabia had no telescopes. The sky most people imagined was
a fixed dome... lights pinned to it, wheeling around a still earth. Into
that world came sentences that moved everything."
Visual: ancient stone sundial or still desert monoliths at night.

4. QUOTE - Qur'an 21:33 (shown)
"And it is He who created the night and the day, and the sun and the moon...
all in an orbit... swimming."
Visual: the moon gliding slowly behind thin clouds.

5. QUOTE - Qur'an 36:40 (shown)
"And again, in the chapter Ya-Sin... It is not allowable for the sun to
reach the moon, nor does the night overtake the day... but each, in an
orbit, is swimming. The Arabic verb is yasbahun... they swim, they glide...
each body carried along its own invisible lane."
Visual: timelapse of sun and moon paths across a wide sky.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"Today we know the moon glides around the earth... the earth around the
sun... the sun itself around the galaxy... nothing in the sky is
nailed anywhere. Everything floats along a path. The classical scholars did
not know the physics... they simply trusted the verb. And the verb held."
Visual: slow drift through a dense starfield.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"Order without pillars... lanes without collisions... for billions of
years. The verse does not ask you to be an astronomer. It asks you to ask...
who is holding all of this in its lanes?"
Visual: serene full night sky over mountains, immense.

### 10. The Forelock That Lied (~2.5 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 96:15-16]
"The Qur'an once described the most arrogant man in Makkah... by a single
patch of his body. Not his heart. Not his tongue. The front of his head...
and it called that patch a liar."
Visual: harsh shadow of a lone figure on a wall, abstract, no face.

2. TITLE CARD - THE FORELOCK / two verses about one tyrant (deep read)
"This is the verse... of the lying forelock."
Visual: slow smoke curling through a shaft of dark red light.

3. [Sahih Muslim 2797]
"Meet the man first. Abu Jahl... a chief of Makkah, the fiercest enemy the
Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, had. The Prophet would pray openly at
the Ka'bah, the ancient sanctuary at the heart of the city... and Abu Jahl
swore before his people that if he saw him pray again, he would trample his
neck."
Visual: torchlit ancient stone columns, ominous mood.

4. QUOTE - Qur'an 96:15-16 (shown)
"And the answer came down from heaven, in the very chapter whose first word
was Read. No! If he does not desist, We will surely drag him by the
forelock... a lying, sinning forelock."
Visual: fist of storm cloud dragging across a dark sky.

5. [Tafsir Ibn Kathir]
"The forelock... the nasiyah... is the front of the head, where the hair
falls over the brow. In Arabic, to be dragged by it is total humiliation...
the proud head, seized from the front. And the classical scholars explain
the description... lying in its statements, sinful in its actions... the
forelock standing for the man himself."
Visual: wind tearing dust off a cliff edge.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"Some readers today pause at a further layer... that behind the forehead
sits the part of the brain most tied to planning, choice, and deceit. Take
that as a quiet reflection, not as the verse's claim... the tafsir needed no
anatomy. The Qur'an aimed at the seat of arrogance... and arrogance has an
address."
Visual: close texture of furrowed stone, brooding light.

7. (context)
"As for Abu Jahl... he never did trample that neck. He died at Badr, the
first great battle, defeated by the people he had tortured... a humiliation
as public as his threat had been."
Visual: aftermath-quiet battlefield landscape at dusk, empty.

8. CLOSE (reflection)
"The forelock is where pride lives... the head you refuse to lower. Muslims
touch theirs to the ground, on purpose, many times a day. The question the
verse leaves behind... is what your forehead bows to."
Visual: prayer mat in warm window light, no person.

### 11. The Verse of Honey (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 16:69]
"There is exactly one food in the Qur'an that Allah calls a healing. Not a
blessing... not a provision. A healing. And it comes out of the body of an
insect."
Visual: golden honey pouring in slow motion, backlit.

2. TITLE CARD - THE HONEY / a healing named in the Qur'an (deep read)
"This is the verse... of the honey."
Visual: slow amber light refracting on black.

3. (context)
"The sixteenth chapter of the Qur'an is named an-Nahl... The Bee. In it,
Allah describes inspiring the bee to build homes in the mountains and
trees, to feed from every fruit... and then to produce something that was
never for the bee alone."
Visual: bees working wildflowers in golden light.

4. QUOTE - Qur'an 16:69 (shown)
"There emerges from their bellies a drink, varying in colors... in which
there is healing for people. Indeed in that is a sign... for a people who
give thought."
Visual: honeycomb close-up, hexagons glowing amber.

5. [Sahih al-Bukhari 5684]
"And the Prophet, peace be upon him, staked a cure on that verse. A man
came to him saying... my brother's stomach is ailing. He said... give him
honey. The man came back... it grew worse. Again... honey. Until the
Prophet said... Allah has spoken the truth, and your brother's stomach has
lied. He drank it... and he was cured."
Visual: tea-colored honey stirred into a glass cup, steam.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"The verse says healing for people... it never said every disease, and
Muslims do not read it as a replacement for medicine. But fourteen
centuries later, honey sits in modern wound clinics and pharmacology
journals... the pantry food found still edible in tombs thousands of years
old... still earning its verse."
Visual: jars of honey in warm window light.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"Indeed in that is a sign for a people who give thought. The bee cannot
know what it makes. The question the chapter asks is quieter... do you know
who taught it?"
Visual: a bee lifting off a flower into soft light.

### 12. When Mosques Become Trophies (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sunan Abi Dawud 449]
"Some prophecies predict disasters. This one predicts something that looks,
from the outside... like success. Golden domes. Record-breaking minarets.
And a warning hiding inside the glitter."
Visual: ornate golden dome gleaming at sunset, exterior only.

2. TITLE CARD - THE BOAST / a sign inside the beautiful (deep read)
"This is the sign... of the boast."
Visual: slow gold filigree light patterns on black.

3. (context)
"A mosque... masjid in Arabic... is simply a place of prostration, where
Muslims pray. The Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, prayed in one built
of mud brick and palm trunks, with a roof that leaked when it rained. From
inside that simplicity... he saw what was coming."
Visual: simple mud-brick courtyard, palm shadows.

4. [Sunan Abi Dawud 449]
"The Hour will not be established... he said... until people boast about
their mosques. Narrated by his servant Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with
him... recorded in Sunan Abi Dawud, and graded authentic."
Visual: towering ornate minarets, low dramatic angle.

5. [Sunan an-Nasa'i 689]
"Another collection carries the same warning with one sharper word...
people will SHOW OFF in building mosques. The building is not the sign...
the showing off is. My dome against your dome. My city's skyline against
yours."
Visual: glittering chandeliers and gilded ceiling, tilt-up.

6. [Sahih al-Bukhari 450]
"The precision matters. Building mosques is beloved in Islam... the
Prophet, peace be upon him, promised a house in Paradise for it. The sign
is not the marble. It is the motive... when houses built for humility
become monuments to pride, while the prayer rows inside them thin out."
Visual: vast empty prayer hall, beams of light, no people.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"He worshipped under a leaking roof and changed the world. The prophecy
asks a question every generation answers for itself... is the beauty for
Allah... or for the photograph?"
Visual: simple prayer rug in a plain sunlit room.

### 13. When Time Speeds Up (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Sahih al-Bukhari 7061]
"Ask anyone over thirty and they will say it like a confession... the years
are getting faster. Fourteen hundred years ago, that exact feeling was
named... as a sign of the end."
Visual: city timelapse, streaking lights, day to night.

2. TITLE CARD - THE SHRINKING TIME / a sign you can feel (deep read)
"This is the sign... of the shrinking time."
Visual: slow spinning star trails on deep black.

3. (context)
"The Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, gave his companions a list of
what the last stretch of history would feel like. Not just wars and
wonders... but how ordinary days would FEEL. And the first feeling on one
famous list... is time itself."
Visual: sand pouring steadily off a dune crest.

4. [Sahih al-Bukhari 7061]
"Time will pass rapidly... he said... good deeds will decrease...
miserliness will be thrown into hearts... and afflictions will appear. It
is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari... and it reads like a diagnosis of a
century he never saw."
Visual: fast clouds racing over a static mountain.

5. QUOTE - Jami at-Tirmidhi 2332 (shown, graded)
"Another narration paints the acceleration in detail. The Hour will not be
established until time is constricted... the year like a month... the month
like a week... the week like a day... the day like an hour... and the hour
like the flare of a fire. A flare... the moment you see it, it is gone."
Visual: a match-strike flare blooming and dying in darkness.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"The scholars offered several readings... blessing draining out of time...
life actually compressing... or hearts too distracted to feel the hours
pass. The narration does not choose for us. It only promises that when the
end approaches... time will feel like it is running away. Does it?"
Visual: fast-forward crowds blurring through a station-free plaza, abstract.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"You cannot slow the river. But anyone who has stood in prayer at dawn
knows some minutes still run deep... remembrance slows the heart, even when
nothing slows the clock. Slow minutes... are still for sale. Barely anyone
is buying."

### 15. And He Is Its Expander (~2 min)

1. HOOK [Qur'an 51:47]
"There is a verse about the sky that got bigger on its own. For thirteen
centuries it meant one majestic thing. Then we built telescopes... and

Visual: immense night sky arching over a tiny lit tent.

2. TITLE CARD - MUSI'UN / the One who makes vast (deep read)
"This is the verse... of the expander."
Visual: slow deep-space-style drift through faint stars.

3. (context)
"The Qur'an speaks constantly about the heaven... as a building, raised
without pillars, held without effort. And in the fifty-first chapter, it
describes the builder... in the present tense."
Visual: canyon walls opening to a river of stars above.

4. QUOTE - Qur'an 51:47 (shown)
"And the heaven We constructed with strength... and indeed, We are [its]
expander."
Visual: clouds peeling back to reveal depth of stars.

5. [Tafsir Ibn Kathir]
"The classical scholars read the Arabic word musi'un through its roots...
vastness and capacity. Ibn Kathir wrote... We made it vast, and raised its
roof without pillars. Others heard the root of power... He is well able,
His capacity absorbs all things. The heaven is immense... because its
Maker's ability is."
Visual: vast aerial of earth's curve-like horizon at high altitude.

6. (reflection, careful framing)
"Then, in the last century, astronomers found that the heavens are not a
finished ceiling... the galaxies are receding, the vastness is still being
made vaster, this very second. Some contemporary readers hear that in this
verse. The classical meaning never needed it... but the verse lost nothing
by the discovery. It simply had room."
Visual: telescope-style deep field of galaxies, slow zoom.

7. CLOSE (reflection)
"With strength We built it... and We are its expander. Thirteen centuries
of readers bowed to a vast sky. You get to bow to a vaster one. The verse
was always big enough for both of you."
Visual: a lone figure-free desert plain under the Milky Way.
