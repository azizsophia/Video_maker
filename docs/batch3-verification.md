## 1. The Splitting of the Moon - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citations:** Qur'an 54:1; Sahih al-Bukhari 3636.
- **Qur'an 54:1 (Sahih International, verified):** "The Hour has come near, and
  the moon has split [in two]."
- **Bukhari 3636 (verified wording):** "During the lifetime of the Prophet
  (peace be upon him) the moon was split into two parts and on that the Prophet
  said, 'Bear witness (to this).'" Narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud. Agreed upon
  (also in Sahih Muslim).
- **Which candidate numbers actually carry the event (all confirmed):**
  - Bukhari 3636 - Ibn Mas'ud.
  - Bukhari 3869 - Ibn Mas'ud: "The moon was split while we were with the
    Prophet in Mina. He said, 'Be witnesses.' Then a piece of the moon went
    towards the mountain." (The eyewitness "we were with him" wording.)
  - Bukhari 4864 - Ibn Mas'ud (tafsir of Surat al-Qamar): one part remained
    over the mountain, one part went beyond it; "Witness this miracle."
  - Bukhari 4865 - Ibn Mas'ud ("it became two parts").
  - Bukhari 4866 - Ibn Abbas ("the moon was cleft asunder during the lifetime
    of the Prophet").
  - Bukhari 4867 and 4868 - Anas ("the people of Mecca asked the Prophet to
    show them a sign, so he showed them the cleaving of the moon").
- **Grading:** Sahih, agreed upon (Bukhari and Muslim), mass-transmitted from
  multiple Companions.
- **Caveats to honor:** The eyewitness narration is Ibn Mas'ud's (he was
  present at Mina). Anas and Ibn Abbas narrate the event but were young
  children or not yet born when it happened in Mecca, so if the script names a
  witness, name Ibn Mas'ud. Anas's narration adds the context that the Meccans
  themselves demanded the sign - attribute that detail to Bukhari 4867.

## 2. Describing Jerusalem after the Night Journey - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citations:** Sahih al-Bukhari 3886; Sahih Muslim 170.
- **Bukhari 3886 (verified wording):** "When the people of Quraish did not
  believe me (i.e. the story of my Night Journey), I stood up in Al-Hijr and
  Allah displayed Jerusalem in front of me, and I began describing it to them
  while I was looking at it." Narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah.
- **Muslim 170 (verified wording):** "When the Quraish belied me, I was
  staying in Hatim and Allah lifted before me Bait-ul-Maqdis and I began to
  narrate to them its signs while I was in fact looking at it." Same Companion
  (Jabir), same event.
- **Grading:** Sahih, agreed upon.
- **Caveats:** "Al-Hijr" and "Hatim" are the same spot (the semicircular area
  by the Ka'bah) - pick one term and gloss it for newcomers. Do not embellish
  with details of what he described (caravan details etc. come from other,
  longer seerah narrations - if used, label them as seerah, not as this
  hadith).

## 3. Barefoot shepherds competing in tall buildings - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citation:** Sahih Muslim 8 (the hadith of Jibril, narrated by
  Umar ibn al-Khattab).
- **Verified key wording (Muslim 8):** among the signs of the Hour "...you
  will find barefooted, destitute goat herders vying with one another in the
  construction of magnificent buildings."
- **Grading:** Sahih Muslim - sahih by definition; the hadith of Jibril is one
  of the most famous authentic hadiths in the religion.
- **CAVEAT (attribution precision, non-negotiable):** The "barefoot,
  destitute" phrasing belongs to Sahih Muslim 8. Bukhari 50 (Abu Hurayrah's
  version of the Jibril hadith) was checked and its wording is DIFFERENT:
  "when the shepherds of black camels start boasting and competing with others
  in the construction of taller buildings." Do not splice the two. If the
  script says "barefoot," the on-screen source must read Sahih Muslim 8, not
  Bukhari 50.

## 4. Following past nations "into a lizard's hole" - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citation:** Sahih al-Bukhari 7320.
- **Verified wording:** "You will follow the ways of those nations who were
  before you, span by span and cubit by cubit (i.e., inch by inch) so much so
  that even if they entered a hole of a mastigure, you would follow them."
  Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. Bukhari 3456 is the same hadith from the
  same Companion; either number is correct, 7320 confirmed verbatim.
- **Grading:** Sahih, agreed upon (Bukhari and Muslim).
- **Caveats:** "Mastigure" is the dhabb, a desert lizard - the script may say
  "lizard's hole," which matches the standard translation. The hadith's own
  continuation ("The Jews and the Christians?" He said, "Whom else?") is part
  of the narration and may be quoted, but deliver it matter-of-factly.

## 5. Wealth so abundant no one accepts charity - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citations:** Sahih al-Bukhari 1411 (the charity-wanderer
  wording) and/or Sahih al-Bukhari 1412 (the explicit "Hour" wording).
- **Bukhari 1411 (verified):** "O people! Give in charity as a time will come
  upon you when a person will wander about with his object of charity and will
  not find anybody to accept it, and one will say, 'If you had brought it
  yesterday I would have taken it, but today I am not in need of it.'"
  Narrated by Harithah ibn Wahb. Agreed upon.
- **Bukhari 1412 (verified):** "The Hour will not be established till your
  wealth increases so much so that one will be worried, for no one will accept
  his Zakat and the person to whom he will give it will reply, 'I am not in
  need of it.'" Narrated by Abu Hurayrah. Agreed upon.
- **Grading:** Both sahih, agreed upon.
- **CAVEAT:** Bukhari 1411 by itself never says "the Hour" - it says "a time
  will come." If the beat frames this as a sign of the Hour, the on-screen
  citation must be Bukhari 1412 (or both numbers). Do not put the 1412
  "Hour" framing over the 1411 number.

## 6. "This matter will reach wherever night and day reach" - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citation:** Musnad Ahmad 16957 (Tamim al-Dari). The candidate
  number 16344 proved WRONG in the sunnah.com / ar-Risala numbering - the
  narration sits at Ahmad 16957. (If a secondary reference is wanted:
  al-Albani, as-Silsilah as-Sahihah no. 3.)
- **Verified wording:** "This matter (Islam) will reach as far as the night
  and day reach, and Allah will not leave a house of mud or a house of hair
  (i.e. settled or nomad) but He will cause this religion to enter it, by the
  might of the mighty or the humiliation of the humbled: might by which Allah
  honors Islam, and humiliation by which He disgraces disbelief." Narrator:
  Tamim al-Dari.
- **Grading:** Solidly authentic - al-Albani graded it sahih (as-Silsilah
  as-Sahihah no. 3, the third hadith of the entire collection); Shu'ayb
  al-Arna'ut graded its chain sound in the Musnad tahqiq; al-Hakim/adh-Dhahabi
  authenticated versions of it. NOT cut.
- **Caveats:** Attribute to Musnad Ahmad (it is not in Bukhari or Muslim).
  Tamim al-Dari's personal comment appended to the narration ("I have seen
  this in my own household...") is the Companion's remark, not the Prophet's
  words - keep the quote boundary clean.

## 7. Salam given only to acquaintances as a sign of the Hour - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citation:** Musnad Ahmad 3848 (Ibn Mas'ud). (Secondary:
  al-Albani, as-Silsilah as-Sahihah no. 647.)
- **Verified wording:** "Among the signs of the Hour is that a man will give
  the greeting (salam) to another only because he knows him" (variant: "the
  greeting will be given only to acquaintances"), in a narration that also
  lists the spread of trade, the severing of family ties, and false testimony.
- **Grading:** Solid - Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut graded the Ahmad chain hasan; the
  Musnad commentators class the fuller Ibn Mas'ud narration sound (IslamQA
  334348 cites Ahmad 6/415-416); al-Albani ruled it sahih in as-Silsilah
  as-Sahihah no. 647 (reported by Ahmad and al-Hakim). NOT cut.
- **CAVEATS:** Attribute to Musnad Ahmad, not to Bukhari/Muslim. Because the
  grading is hasan/sahih rather than agreed-upon, the script should quote the
  sign itself plainly and not stack extra details from weaker variants (some
  circulating versions bundle in extra signs from weaker chains - use only the
  greeting/trade/family-ties/false-testimony core that the graded narration
  carries).

## 8. Qur'an 21:32 - the sky as a protected ceiling - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citation:** Qur'an 21:32.
- **Verified wording (Sahih International):** "And We made the sky a protected
  ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away."
- **Classical framing (Ibn Kathir, verified on quran.com):** the sky is a
  roof/dome over the earth, "safe and well-guarded... high and protected from
  anything reaching it"; Mujahid glosses mahfuzan as "raised up." Classical
  tafsir also connects protection with the guarding of the heaven from
  devils/shayatin (as in 15:17 and 41:12).
- **Grading:** Qur'an - no grading needed; translation verified.
- **CAVEATS (framing limit):** Present the classical meaning first (a guarded,
  raised roof). The modern observation (atmosphere/magnetosphere shielding
  earth from radiation and meteoroids) may be offered as a separate reflective
  layer, worded as "today we know the sky above us shields us from..." - never
  as "this verse predicted the magnetosphere." Do not overclaim. Show the
  verse Arabic from Quran.com; never recite it with AI voice.

## 9. Qur'an 21:33 and 36:40 - "each in an orbit, swimming" - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citations:** Qur'an 21:33 and Qur'an 36:40.
- **21:33 (Sahih International, verified):** "And it is He who created the
  night and the day and the sun and the moon; all [heavenly bodies] in an
  orbit are swimming."
- **36:40 (Sahih International, verified):** "It is not allowable for the sun
  to reach the moon, nor does the night overtake the day, but each, in an
  orbit, is swimming."
- **Grading:** Qur'an; wording verified against the alquran.cloud Sahih
  International text.
- **Caveats:** "[heavenly bodies]" in 21:33 is a translator's bracket - if
  quoted on screen, keep the brackets or trim to the unbracketed clause. The
  Arabic verb yasbahun ("they swim/glide") may be glossed, since the channel
  translates every Arabic term on first use.

## 10. Qur'an 96:15-16 - "a lying, sinning forelock" - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citation:** Qur'an 96:15-16.
- **Verified wording (Sahih International):** "No! If he does not desist, We
  will surely drag him by the forelock - A lying, sinning forelock."
- **Classical framing (Ibn Kathir, verified):** the verses were revealed about
  Abu Jahl, who threatened the Prophet at prayer; "the forelock of Abu Jahl -
  lying in its statements and sinful in its actions." The nasiyah (forelock,
  the front of the head) stands for the man himself - a standard Arabic
  metonymy; being dragged by the forelock is an image of utter humiliation.
- **Grading:** Qur'an; translation verified.
- **CAVEATS (hard framing limit):** The channel does NOT run the "prefrontal
  cortex miracle" claim. Classical tafsir reads "lying, sinning" as describing
  the person (Abu Jahl), not as an anatomy lesson. If a modern layer is used
  at all, it is at most a light, separate reflection ("the seat of planning
  and choice sits behind the forehead"), clearly framed as reflection, not as
  the verse's claim, and without the word "neuroscience proves." Also keep the
  historical context: the passage is about Abu Jahl's threat, so the script
  should introduce him (Meccan chief, enemy of the Prophet) per the
  newcomer-first rule.

## 11. Honey healing - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citations:** Qur'an 16:69; Sahih al-Bukhari 5684.
- **16:69 (Sahih International, verified, key clause):** "There emerges from
  their bellies a drink, varying in colors, in which there is healing for
  people. Indeed in that is a sign for a people who give thought."
- **Bukhari 5684 (verified):** a man came to the Prophet saying "My brother
  has some abdominal trouble"; the Prophet told him repeatedly to give him
  honey; after the man kept returning, the Prophet said, "Allah has said the
  truth, but your brother's abdomen has told a lie. Let him drink honey." He
  drank it and was cured. Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. Bukhari 5716 is the
  same incident, same Companion - both numbers confirmed correct; use 5684 as
  primary.
- **Grading:** Sahih al-Bukhari; also in Muslim (agreed upon).
- **Caveats:** The verse says "healing for people," not "cure for every
  disease" - keep the claim exactly as sourced. The hadith is about one man
  with a stomach ailment; do not generalize it into medical advice beyond
  what the texts say.

## 12. Boasting/competing in mosques as a sign of the Hour - VERDICT: CLEARED

- **On-screen citation:** Sunan Abi Dawud 449 (secondary: Sunan an-Nasa'i 689).
- **Abu Dawud 449 (verified wording):** "The Hour will not be established
  until people boast about (their) mosques." Narrated by Anas ibn Malik.
- **An-Nasa'i 689 (verified wording):** "One of the portents of the Hour will
  be that people will show off in building masjids." Same Companion (Anas),
  same hadith.
- **Grading:** Sahih - al-Albani graded Abu Dawud 449 sahih (confirmed on the
  mirror: "Sound (Albani)"); an-Nasa'i 689 graded sahih (Zubair Ali Zai;
  al-Albani likewise authenticated it in Sahih an-Nasa'i). NOT cut.
- **Caveats:** The verb is "boast/show off/vie" (yatabaha) - rivalry and
  ostentation in mosque building, not building or beautifying mosques per se.
  The script must not imply that building mosques is itself blameworthy.

## 13. Time contracting / passing quickly - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **Primary on-screen citation:** Sahih al-Bukhari 7061. **Optional second:**
  Jami at-Tirmidhi 2332 (with grading shown).
- **Bukhari 7061 (verified wording):** "Time will pass rapidly, good deeds
  will decrease, miserliness will be thrown (in the hearts of the people),
  afflictions will appear and there will be much al-Harj." They said, "What is
  al-Harj?" He said, "Killing! Killing!" Narrated by Abu Hurayrah. Agreed upon.
- **Tirmidhi 2332 (verified wording):** "The Hour shall not be established
  until time is constricted, and the year is like a month, and the month is
  like the week, and the week is like the day, and the day is like the hour,
  and the hour is like the flare of a fire." Narrated by Anas ibn Malik.
- **Grading:** Bukhari 7061 - sahih, agreed upon; carry the beat on this one.
  Tirmidhi 2332 - at-Tirmidhi himself said "gharib from this chain"; al-Albani
  graded it sahih (Sahih at-Tirmidhi / Sahih al-Jami), and Zubair Ali Zai
  graded it sound. Usable, but it is the vivid poetic elaboration, not the
  bedrock.
- **CAVEATS:** Stand the claim on Bukhari 7061. If the year/month/week/day
  imagery is used, cite Tirmidhi 2332 on screen for that quote specifically -
  do not run Tirmidhi wording over a Bukhari citation. Scholars read taqarub
  az-zaman variously (blessing leaving time, actual acceleration, perceived
  speed); the script should quote the text and let it stand, without picking
  one speculative interpretation as fact.

## 14. The conquest of Constantinople prophecy - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS (the famous "excellent amir" wording is NOT cleared)

- **RECOMMENDED on-screen citation: Sahih Muslim 2920** (with Muslim 2897 as a
  supporting explicit mention). Do NOT put Musnad Ahmad 18957 on screen.
- **The honest finding on the famous wording** ("You will certainly conquer
  Constantinople; what an excellent amir will her amir be, and what an
  excellent army that army"), Musnad Ahmad 18957 / al-Bukhari's at-Tarikh
  al-Kabir / al-Hakim:
  - Authenticated by: al-Hakim (sahih isnad), adh-Dhahabi concurred;
    al-Haythami relayed at-Tabarani's chain as sahih; Ibn Abd al-Barr called a
    chain hasan.
  - Weakened by: Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut (in the Musnad tahqiq at 18957), al-Albani,
    and Ahmad Shakir. The pivot of the weakness: the narrator Ubayd (b. Bishr,
    also given as Abd Allah b. Bishr al-Khath'ami) is majhul (unknown), with
    single-strand transmission above and below him.
  - Bottom line: the grading is genuinely contested; by this channel's bar
    (solid sahih/hasan only) the "excellent amir" wording does not clear.
    CUT the "excellent amir" quote from scripts. At most, narration may say
    "and in another narration, whose grading scholars dispute, the conqueror
    and his army are praised" - and even that is better omitted.
- **What DOES clear (verified):**
  - **Sahih Muslim 2920** (Abu Hurayrah): "Have you heard of a city, one side
    of which is on land and the other is in the sea?" They said: "Yes,
    Messenger of Allah." He said: "The Last Hour will not come until seventy
    thousand of the Banu Ishaq attack it... they will not fight with weapons
    nor shower arrows but will say 'There is no god but Allah and Allah is the
    Greatest' and one side of it will fall... then the gates will be opened
    for them... and while distributing the spoils they will hear the cry:
    'The Dajjal has come out,' and they will leave everything and return."
  - **Sahih Muslim 2897** (Abu Hurayrah, the Malahim hadith) explicitly names
    the city: "...and they will conquer Constantinople (Qustantiniyyah)."
- **CAVEATS (framing, non-negotiable):** In Muslim 2920 the name
  "Constantinople" is the translator's/commentators' identification - the matn
  describes "a city one side on land, one side on sea"; Muslim 2897 is where
  the name appears explicitly, so pair the numbers accordingly. Both Muslim
  narrations describe an end-times conquest tied to the emergence of the
  Dajjal (takbir instead of weapons, Banu Ishaq), which does NOT match the
  Ottoman conquest of 1453 - the script must not claim "this hadith was
  fulfilled in 1453." The safe, honest line: the Prophet foretold that
  Constantinople would fall to the Muslims (Muslim 2897), and history records
  Muslims striving toward it for centuries until 1453, while the fuller
  description in Muslim 2920 points to an event still to come. Deliver the
  precision matter-of-factly per the tone rule - no "we checked."

## 15. Qur'an 51:47 - "We are its expander" - VERDICT: CLEARED WITH CAVEATS

- **On-screen citation:** Qur'an 51:47.
- **Verified wording (Sahih International):** "And the heaven We constructed
  with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander." Note the translator's
  bracket around "[its]". Other majors: Pickthall "We it is Who make the vast
  extent (thereof)"; Yusuf Ali "it is We Who create the vastness of space";
  literal la-musi'un = "surely making vast / possessors of vastness-ability."
- **Classical tafsir (verified, Ibn Kathir on quran.com):** "We made it vast
  and We brought its roof higher without pillars"; classical readings of
  musi'un cluster on (a) vastness - He made the heaven immense, and (b) power
  and capacity - from wus' (ability), i.e. He is well able; some add expanding
  provision. The classical meaning is vastness/power, not a cosmological
  process claim.
- **Grading:** Qur'an; translation verified.
- **CAVEATS (framing limit, per the channel's rule):** Classical layer FIRST
  (built with strength, made vast, He is the possessor of all capacity). The
  modern cosmological reading (an expanding universe) is presented as a
  separate, later layer - "some contemporary readers hear in this verse..." -
  never as "the Qur'an stated Hubble expansion." Keep translator brackets
  intact on screen. Arabic text from Quran.com, shown not recited.
